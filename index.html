How the analysis works

  1. Intensity is divided by laser power × integration time. Bad CCD pixels and 1 to 2 pixel cosmic-ray spikes are replaced, keeping narrow real peaks such as Si.
  2. Si calibration fits a Lorentzian plus a linear background to the Si peak and shifts the whole Raman shift axis so Si lands on the reference value. Every spectrum then shares the same calibration.
  3. A noise-aware adaptive baseline is subtracted, following the Empa procedure (Lüthi et al.): structured peaks are masked, anchors are taken from background regions, anchors that sit above the baseline are removed, and the baseline is shifted slightly below the signal. Because Lorentzian tails reach far from each peak, a smooth spline background is then fitted together with all peaks, so tails are not absorbed into the baseline and the background uncertainty enters the error bars.
  4. The fixed model fits every listed peak, including small shoulders such as 7-LA/6-LO (b), 8-iTO and the G-like modes, as in Yumigeta et al. By default the spectrum is split into regions (RBLM, Si, CH/D, G) and each region is fitted with all its peaks plus a local linear background, from several starting guesses, keeping the best. Each peak stays within its ± window and FWHM range. The adaptive model instead adds peaks it detects and removes weak, nested or limit-bound ones, as in the Empa workflow.
  5. Fitting uses a global grid search followed by Levenberg-Marquardt refinement of all peaks together. The fitting engine was checked against SciPy's least_squares on 243 parameters from 9 real spectra, agreeing to better than 0.02%.
  6. Uncertainties come either from the fit covariance or, by default, from a Monte Carlo of the whole pipeline: noise matching each spectrum is added to its fitted model, the full analysis is repeated, and the spread of each result is reported. This includes baseline and peak-overlap uncertainty that the covariance misses.
  7. Normalization divides spectra, heights and areas by the G band maximum (as in Fig. 2a of Yumigeta et al.) or by a fitted peak height. It updates instantly and leaves ratios unchanged.

Numeric settings are our choices and are written into the methods file so they can be reported.

Methods implemented: the adaptive baseline and adaptive refitting follow Lüthi et al., arXiv:2606.23304 (2026); the peak model and regional fitting follow Yumigeta et al., ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 18 (2026) 25103–25111, doi:10.1021/acsami.6c02516.
